# Editing judgments

All examples below are constructed illustrations, not quotations or findings from
the user's manuscript. Use their reasoning, not their wording as a template.

| Source or issue | Useful revision or decision | Harmful change |
|---|---|---|
| “Selection-induced estimation bias constitutes an impediment to value assessment.” The supplied analysis explains selection on favorable errors. | “We may overestimate the selected treatment's value because treatments with favorable estimation errors are more likely to be selected.” The mechanism is explicit and source-supported. | Add this mechanism when the source does not establish it. |
| “The chosen action need not be optimal, and its value can still be overestimated.” | Keep selected and optimal distinct, even if “selected” repeats nearby. | Replace “chosen” with “best” for variety. |
| “Under Assumptions A–C, the estimator is consistent as the sample size grows.” | Preserve the assumptions and asymptotic scope while simplifying surrounding prose. | “The estimator is accurate in any sample.” |
| “Treatment and retention were positively associated in the observational sample.” | Keep the association, design, and population visible. | “Treatment increased retention.” |
| “Bias fell, but variance rose enough that mean squared error increased.” | Retain both components and the overall comparison. | “The method improved accuracy.” |
| “Rates were 3.0% and 2.4%; the first exceeded the second by 0.6 percentage points.” | If useful, explain that the relative increase is 25% using 2.4% as the denominator. | Call the difference a 0.6% relative increase. |
| “The diagnostic uses observed stability. We evaluate MAE using known simulation truth.” | Keep tuning inputs separate from evaluation quantities. | “Practitioners should choose the parameter minimizing MAE” when truth is unavailable. |
| “We randomly assigned 600 participants to one of three groups.” | Leave it unchanged if it fits the context. | “A randomized allocation procedure was implemented across a tripartite grouping.” |
| Methods say 0.4; the caption says 0.7 for the same parameter. | Flag the conflict and request the authoritative value. Preserve both until resolved. | Pick either value because it appears more plausible. |
| Notes ask whether learning from past proofs can guide theorem search; the prototype ranks candidate lemmas for one proof system. An introduction calls this “a minor ranking adjustment.” | “We investigate whether learning from past proofs can guide theorem search. Our prototype studies this idea by ranking candidate lemmas in one proof system.” The framing restores the supplied question while retaining the demonstration's scope. | Claim the prototype automates mathematical discovery generally, or retain the minor framing solely to avoid possible objections. |
| In a laboratory task, 180 participants were randomly assigned to layouts A or B. Mean error counts were 2.1 and 3.4, respectively. Reduced distraction is a proposed, unmeasured explanation. | “Participants assigned to layout A made fewer errors on average than those assigned to B (2.1 versus 3.4). Reduced distraction may explain this difference.” Preserve the sample and laboratory context in surrounding prose. | Hedge the observed means as though they were unknown, or claim the study established reduced distraction as the mechanism. |
| “Although we assume independent arrivals, this assumption makes the queueing analysis tractable.” The intended relationship is analytical purpose. | “We assume independent arrivals to make the queueing analysis tractable.” | Retain a concession where the source establishes no tension, or infer that “although” is always undesirable. |
| “We conducted sensitivity checks under four covariance structures.” The supplied results show that the estimated effect retained its direction and a similar magnitude. | “The estimated effect was robust to the four covariance structures examined.” Give the detailed comparisons where useful. | Merely announce the checks when their result is the point, or claim robustness when the checks instead reveal material instability. |
| “Participants were randomly assigned at random to one of three groups.” The probability of assignment is not at issue. | Remove “at random.” Explain allocation probabilities separately if readers need them. | Add further bookkeeping to make the sentence look precise, or remove a consequential allocation rule in the name of brevity. |
| A technical convention is underexplained in the main text, while a long secondary proof interrupts the central result. | Add enough explanation to interpret the convention, perhaps in a short note. If structural revision is authorized, give the proof a coherent location with a clear pointer. | Apply “shorter is better” to both passages, or move a condition needed to understand the main claim into an appendix. |

## Basis and limits

The design follows the supplied reader-centered writing guidance: make claims,
reasoning, evidence, and conditions recoverable; distinguish meaning constraints
from stylistic preferences; and demonstrate restraint as well as transformation.
These illustrations are guidance, not evidence that the skill outperforms a strong
plain prompt. Evaluate new inputs separately from development examples.
